UNMAPPED · 無界之地 · Sepolia · ETHGlobal Tokyo 2026

ENS names the family tree. Uniswap prices it. A passkey owns it.

Names come first: every cartridge gets an ENSv2 name under the world it was remixed from, and every player's save gets one under its cartridge, held by the player's own passkey account. A named world can then be launched: a token sold in a Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction and — once the auction graduates — a v4 pool whose hook pays royalties up the family tree to whoever holds each name. Players never connect a wallet, and the app holds no key: their passkey signs, a gas station pays.

How the pieces talk

Four places run code. The player's side only ever signs; the app's main process builds every batch and keeps its digest but holds no key; a gas station (a Cloudflare Worker) carries it and pays; the contracts on Sepolia check the passkey signature themselves, so the station can carry an action but never change or replay it.

Who gets paid when a remix trades

A remix's token is sold and traded against its parent's token, so the pools form the same tree as the names. Buying a grandchild walks down the line — each hop is a swap in one pool — and every hop takes a 1% royalty from its output and splits it up the family: half to the world traded in that pool, 30% to its parent, 20% to its grandparent. A missing ancestor's share stays with the world.

The life of a world

  1. Publish a cartridge in the app. Its sha256 content hash is its identity; the bytes never go on chain.
  2. Name — LineageRegistry.register (Worlds → Cartridges, one passkey signature): <cartridge>.unmapped.eth, or under its parent's name for a remix, with its own children's registry and the id / version / hash records. The name's holder can point it at later revisions.
  3. Play and record — a player names their own save with recordSave (Worlds → Saves): <save>.<cartridge>.unmapped.eth, held by their passkey account, recording the save's sha256, the pinned version and a progress line; updateSave moves it forward. A backup restored elsewhere finds its name by hash.
  4. Launch — the holder calls launch on the name: mint the token and start the Continuous Clearing Auction through Uniswap's LBPStrategy (priced in the parent's token, or MockUSDC at the top). A remix launches only after its parent.
  5. Bid — each block the auction sells an equal slice of supply at the price where demand meets it. Players bid from their passkey account; the gas station carries it.
  6. Settle — after the last block anyone may exit and claim bids; every filled bid pays the one clearing price.
  7. Graduate — LBPStrategy opens the v4 pool at the discovered price with half of what was raised; LineageHook accepts only this initializer and only a world × parent pairing.
  8. Trade along the family line through LineageRouter; every hop pays the 1% royalty up the tree.
  9. Claim — the hook pays each world's accrued royalty to its current ENS holder; revise points the name at a newer revision (holder only).
  10. Remix — a new cartridge named under this one hangs under its name and, once launched, trades against its token. The loop repeats.

One thing to know before a live demo

Electron cannot show Touch ID for WebAuthn in a development build — that needs app.configureWebAuthn and a keychain entitlement in a signed build. So the app hands the one signature step to the system browser: it opens a localhost page, Chrome or Safari asks for Touch ID, and the signature comes back to the app, which hands it to the gas station. The passkey lives in the player's own keychain; still no wallet. A USB security key also works inside the app. The deployed station needs its key once: wrangler secret put RELAYER_KEY.
